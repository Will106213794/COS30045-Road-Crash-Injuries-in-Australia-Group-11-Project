Where the numbers come from

Three BITRE files, eleven years, more than 90,000 rows. This page explains the source, what each column means, how we cleaned it, and where it falls short. You can also browse and download the data below.

Source

The data comes from the Bureau of Infrastructure and Transport Research Economics (BITRE) Hospitalised Injury publication (BITRE, 2023). BITRE builds it from the Australian Institute of Health and Welfare (AIHW) National Hospital Morbidity Database, which records every hospital admission in Australia.

We use the national file (hospitalisation_injury_publication_sep2023.xlsx), raw tab, covering calendar years 2011 to 2021.

What one row means

Each row is one combination of year, month, remoteness area, age group, sex, road user and counterparty (what they crashed with). The Hospitalisations column says how many people were admitted for that combination, and Bed days says how many days they spent in hospital in total.

What "n.p." means

"Not published": BITRE hides very small counts to protect privacy. They are blank in the CSV. The site never treats them as zero; charts show them with stripes or hollow points, and tooltips say "includes n.p. values". ACT, NT and Tasmania have the most.

Check before you sum

In the First Nations export, the rows labelled "Overall" are an exact copy of the "Age" rows, so the site leaves them out. Adding every breakdown together would count each person several times; always pick one breakdown.

Cleaning

How we prepared it

  1. In KNIME: read the raw tab, checked for duplicate rows (none found) and missing values, kept Traffic rows only, and removed columns we do not use.
  2. Fixed the age label 8-16, which Excel can turn into the date 16-Aug.
  3. Removed rows with an unknown age or an unknown sex. This is a very small number of hospitalisations (see the counts on the right).
  4. Added two grouping columns: Older group and Road user group.
  5. Removed an invisible character (a byte-order mark) from the first column name, which stopped D3 reading the year column.

Limitations

Counts, not rates

BITRE gives counts, not rates per 100,000. We checked with ABS population figures: people aged 65+ grew by about 42% from 2011 to 2021 and their hospitalisations by 41%, so the rate per older person stayed about the same (about 126 per 100,000). See the Reality check in the story.

COVID-19 in 2020

Lockdowns reduced travel in 2020, so the drop that year is unusual. We point it out on the charts rather than removing it.

Bed days change in 2015

Bed days rise by about a quarter for every age group between 2014 and 2015 while admissions barely change. This may reflect a change in recording, so we compare age groups within the same year.

Deaths in hospital

Hospitalisations leave out people who died in hospital, and the death columns are empty for 2021, so we do not analyse deaths. Bed days may still include some of those stays.
