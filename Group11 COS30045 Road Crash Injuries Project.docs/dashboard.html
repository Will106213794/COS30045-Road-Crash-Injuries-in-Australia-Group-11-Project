Three words before you start

Hospitalisation

One admission to hospital after a road crash. People who died in hospital are not counted here.

Photo: Yassine Khalfalli, Unsplash

Bed day

One day spent in a hospital bed. Ten patients staying 3 days each is 30 bed days, so it shows how serious injuries are.

Photo: Ante Samarzija, Unsplash

Older group

People aged 65 and over: the 65 to 74 and 75+ age groups in the data. Everyone else is "under 65".

Photo: Centre for Ageing Better, Unsplash

Mostly car crashes

Car drivers and passengers are the biggest group every year, for every age group except children aged 8 to 16, where cyclists lead.

Photo: Henrique Felix, Unsplash (Pyrmont, Sydney)

Story guide

…

Why index?

The 40 to 64 group has four to five times more hospitalisations than people aged 65 to 74, so in raw counts the older lines look flat. The growth view shows that the 65 to 74 line actually grew the fastest.

Story guide

Cycling in later life

Older cyclists in hospital more than tripled from 2011 to 2021, the fastest rise of any road user group aged 65+.

Photo: Carlos Del Cura, Unsplash

Back on the bike

Older motorcyclists in hospital more than doubled, mostly men.

Photo: Tao Yuan, Unsplash

Longer recovery

Older people usually take longer to recover. Within the same year, patients aged 65+ stay about twice as long as patients under 65.

Photo: Ante Samarzija, Unsplash

Story guide

Loading the First Nations file…

Uses the BITRE First Nations file, which compares Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people ("First Nations people") with non-Indigenous people. These are counts, not rates. The First Nations population is younger on average, which is part of why fewer patients are aged 65+. The year and measure controls apply; other filters do not.
