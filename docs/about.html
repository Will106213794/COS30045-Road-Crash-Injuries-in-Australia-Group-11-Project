About this project

Road Crash Injuries Across Ages: The Growing Older Group

An interactive story and dashboard showing that Australians aged 65 and over are a growing share of road crash hospitalisations, why that is happening, and what it means for road safety and hospitals. COS30045 Data Visualisation, Group 11.

At a glance

The question

Are people aged 65+ becoming a bigger part of road crash hospitalisations, and who among them?

The data

Three BITRE (2023) Hospitalised Injury files, 2011 to 2021, cleaned in KNIME, plus ABS population for a rate check.

Who it is for

Curious members of the public, road safety and transport planners, councils planning cycle paths, and hospital planners.

Built with

KNIME, D3.js v7, plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, GitHub and GitHub Pages. No frameworks.

How we designed it

Six decisions that shape the site

01

One path, same numbers

Story, then dashboard, then data. The six storylines have the same numbers everywhere, and every chapter links to its dashboard tab.

02

The right chart for each question

Lines for change over time, an index for groups of different size, a dumbbell for before and after, a pyramid for age profiles. See the table below.

03

Older group always stands out

People aged 65+ are always the strongest colour and everyone else is quieter, so the eye goes to the story first.

04

Colours tested, not guessed

Every palette was run through a colour-blindness simulation. Colour is never the only clue: labels, dashes and hatching back it up.

05

Honest numbers

Suppressed (n.p.) values are striped, not hidden. A reality check with ABS population shows the rate per older person stayed about the same.

06

Usable by everyone

Six themes, automatic view size, full keyboard control, tooltips by mouse, touch or Tab, and a data table plus CSV for every chart.

Which chart, and why

QuestionChartWhy this chart
How did the total change?LineBest for a trend over time; the COVID-19 dip is annotated.
Which age group grew fastest?Indexed lines (2011 = 100)Groups differ a lot in size; an index compares growth fairly.
Is the older share growing?Bars of % shareShows the share each year; a pie could not show change over time.
Which road users drive it?Bars of % change with countsCounts are shown beside the % so a big % from a small number is not misread.
Men or women?Two lines, one dashedTwo series over time; the dash works without colour.
How long do people stay?DumbbellShows before and after for every age group in one view.
Is it the same in every state?Dumbbell and heatmapCompares states without a map, where big empty areas would dominate.
How do First Nations age profiles differ?Back-to-back pyramidThe standard way to compare the shape of two age profiles.

More details

Use of generative AI

We used Claude (Anthropic) to help plan the KNIME workflow, draft and debug the D3 and CSS code, check the colour palettes, and draw the background banners and cartoon illustrations. All numbers on this site were checked against the BITRE source data and the team reviewed and edited all text.
